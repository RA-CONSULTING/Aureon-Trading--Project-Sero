## 2. Layer 1: Casimir Cavity — The Vacuum Filter

The innermost boundary is a Casimir cavity: two conducting plates separated by distance *d*. Only electromagnetic modes satisfying λ_n = 2d/n can persist as standing waves. All longer wavelengths are suppressed.

**Experimental basis:**
- **Lamoreaux (1997, *PRL* 78, 5):** First precision Casimir measurement, 5% agreement with theory.
- **Mohideen and Roy (1998, *PRL* 81, 4549):** Refined to 1% accuracy at 100 nm via AFM.
- **Decca et al. (2007, *PRD* 75, 077101):** Pushed to 0.2% total error at ~160 nm using MEMS.
- **Moddel et al. (2021, *PRR* 3, L022007):** Demonstrated mode-filtering in MIMOC devices — conductance altered by suppressing vacuum modes exceeding ~2d.

**Druid application:** A 100 nm gap suppresses all wavelengths >200 nm (UV and below), creating a spectral filter that eliminates the infrared and microwave bands most commonly used for interference.

**Active extension — Dynamical Casimir Effect (DCE):**
- **Wilson et al. (2011, *Nature* 479, 376):** Real microwave photon pairs from modulated SQUID-terminated transmission line.
- **Lähteenmäki et al. (2013, *PNAS* 110, 4234):** 23 dB parametric gain at 5.4 GHz using 250-SQUID Josephson metamaterial.

Druid employs DCE to **actively seed** protective vacuum modes, reinforcing the cavity's filtering function during high-threat periods.

## 3. Layer 2: Plasma Shield — The Reflective Barrier

The second layer is a magnetised plasma sheath that reflects incident electromagnetic radiation through three mechanisms:

**1. Collisional reflection:** Plasma electrons scatter incoming photons, reducing transmission exponentially with plasma density.

**2. Cutoff frequency:** For a plasma frequency ω_p, all radiation with ω < ω_p is totally reflected. A 13.56 MHz RF plasma (standard industrial frequency) has ω_p ≈ 10¹⁰ rad/s, reflecting everything below ~1.6 GHz.

**3. Destructive interference:** A double-layer plasma (two sheaths with π phase offset) produces near-perfect cancellation of transmitted waves.

**Experimental basis:** Plasma EM shielding is well-documented in spacecraft protection (NASA) and fusion reactor wall design (ITER). The 13.56 MHz frequency is chosen for its industrial maturity — power supplies, impedance matchers, and plasma diagnostics are commercially available.

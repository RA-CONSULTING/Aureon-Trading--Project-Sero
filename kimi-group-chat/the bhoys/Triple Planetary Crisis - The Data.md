# Triple Planetary Crisis: The Data

A hard-data briefing on the three biggest environmental threats facing the planet right now. Compiled July 2026.

---

## 1. Climate Change

### Where We Stand
- **2025 was one of the top three warmest years ever recorded.** Global temperatures breached thresholds associated with more severe and frequent extremes.
- **Current global temperature:** ~1.2 degrees C above pre-industrial levels in 2020, projected to reach **2.1 degrees C by 2050** under current policies (OECD Environmental Outlook).
- **2024 was the warmest year on record**, reaching 1.55 degrees C above pre-industrial levels.
- **CO2 emissions hit a new all-time high in 2025**, up 1.1% from 2024 (Berkeley Earth).
- The three-year average now **exceeds the 1.5 degrees C Paris target**.

### Where We Are Headed
- Even if all countries fully implement their current climate pledges (NDCs), global temperatures could still rise by **2.3 to 2.5 degrees C this century** (UNEP 2025 Emissions Gap Report).
- It is now **almost inevitable that 1.5 degrees C will be exceeded** in the near term due to insufficient action (IPCC).
- Returning to 1.5 degrees C by 2100 may still be possible, but requires immediate, deep, sustained emissions cuts plus substantial carbon removal.

### Key Regional Impact
- Land areas warm roughly **twice as fast as oceans**.
- **97% of Earth's surface** was significantly warmer in 2025 than the 1951-1980 average.
- **9.1% of Earth's surface set new local heat records** in 2025, home to approximately **770 million people**.

## 2. Pollution

### The Scale
- **9 million deaths annually** are attributed to pollution, equivalent to **1 in 6 deaths worldwide**.
- **Air pollution:** 36% of the world's population is exposed to PM2.5 levels above the least stringent WHO interim target of 35 ug/m3.
- **Plastic pollution:** Over **400 million tonnes** of plastic produced every year. More than **8 million tonnes** enter the oceans annually.
- **91% of the world's plastic waste is still not recycled.**
- Half of all plastic produced is used just once.
- Without new policy interventions, plastic leakage into the environment is projected to **increase by 64% between 2020 and 2050**.

### Human Cost
- 95% of air pollution-attributable deaths in adults over 60 are from noncommunicable diseases (COPD, dementia, diabetes, heart disease, lung disease).
- Health impacts linked to plastic production and waste are **projected to increase by 75% by 2040**.

### Marine Impact
- Every day, the equivalent of **over 2,000 garbage trucks** full of plastic is dumped into oceans, rivers, and lakes.
- More than **100,000 marine mammals** die from plastic entanglement or ingestion each year.
- The Great Pacific Garbage Patch is approximately **three times the size of France** and still growing.

### Trends
- One positive: global PM2.5 concentrations are projected to **decline by 21% by 2050** under current policies.
- But mismanaged plastic waste and built-up land area show the most significant projected increases, rising by **68% and 49%** respectively by 2050.

## 3. Biodiversity Loss

### The Collapse
- **73% average decline** in monitored wildlife populations between 1970 and 2020 (WWF Living Planet Report 2024).
- **1 in 8 animal and plant species** already face global extinction.
- Approximately **1 million species** face extinction risk across all of Earth's known biodiversity (IPBES).
- The IUCN Red List currently lists **48,646 species as threatened** out of 172,620 assessed.

### Breakdown by Habitat
- **Freshwater populations:** 85% decline (worst hit). 1 in 4 freshwater fauna species threatened.
- **Terrestrial populations:** 69% decline.
- **Marine populations:** 56% decline. 84% of global coral reef systems affected by mass bleaching.

### Breakdown by Region
- **Latin America & Caribbean:** 95% decline since 1970 (worst region).
- **Africa:** 76% decline.
- **Asia-Pacific:** 60% decline.
- **North America:** 39% decline.
- **Europe & Central Asia:** 35% decline.

### Drivers
Historically:
- **Land/sea use change:** 30% of human impact
- **Direct exploitation:** 23%
- **Climate change:** 14%
- **Pollution & invasive species:** remainder

**By 2050, climate change will overtake land use change as the main driver of biodiversity loss**, explaining two-thirds of the projected decline.

### What Is At Stake
- The Amazon holds >10% of Earth's terrestrial biodiversity and stores **250-300 billion tons of carbon** (15-20 years of global GHG emissions).
- If the Amazon tipping point is crossed, up to **75 billion tons of carbon** could be released, rendering the 1.5 degrees C goal impossible.
- Biodiversity loss costs the global economy an estimated **$10 trillion annually** in agriculture, healthcare, and ecosystem service losses.

---

## The Interconnection

These three crises are not separate. They reinforce each other:
- Climate change accelerates biodiversity loss.
- Biodiversity decline reduces carbon sinks, worsening climate change.
- Pollution (especially nutrient and plastic) directly kills species and degrades habitats.
- A February 2026 study in Science Advances confirmed: wildlife facing **multiple simultaneous threats decline faster** than those facing single pressures. Conservation that tackles one problem at a time is structurally insufficient.

---

## Bottom Line

Under current policies, the OECD projects most environmental pressures **rise between 2020 and 2050**:
- Global temperatures: +0.9 degrees C
- Biodiversity loss: +8% globally
- Plastic leakage: +64%
- Nitrogen surplus: +32%
- Built-up land area: +49%

Current policy measures fall short of halting and reversing any of the three crises. The data is unambiguous. The question is what to do about it.

*Sources: OECD Environmental Outlook 2025, IPCC, UNEP Emissions Gap Report 2025, WWF Living Planet Report 2024, IUCN Red List 2025, IPBES Global Assessment, Berkeley Earth 2025, State of Global Air 2025, Science Advances 2026.*

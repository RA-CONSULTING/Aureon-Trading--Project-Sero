## Tier 5: The Backtrackers — Post-Paris Betrayal

In the 7 years after the Paris Agreement (2016-2022), **62 corporate/state entities** INCREASED emissions compared to the 7 years before (2009-2015).

| Region | % of Companies Increasing Emissions | Avg Change |
|--------|-------------------------------------|------------|
| Middle East | 7/10 | +12.4% |
| Asia | 13/15 | +7.4% |
| Africa | 3/6 | +7.7% |
| Europe | 13/23 | +4.1% |
| South America | 3/5 | -1.4% (Venezuela decline offset) |
| Australia | 3/4 | -10.2% (BHP decrease offset) |
| North America | 16/37 | **-16.0%** (coal decline driven) |

Only North America saw a majority of companies decrease emissions — and that was driven by coal decline, not oil/gas.

## What This Means

1. **36 companies = half the problem.** If these entities were forced to transition, global emissions would drop dramatically.
2. **Fines are theater.** A $1.6B fine against Hino sounds big, but it's a cost of doing business. Valero's $82M in Bay Area fines didn't stop them from accumulating 270+ new violations.
3. **Greenwashing is the new normal.** Nearly every major corporation is retreating from climate commitments while maintaining eco-friendly marketing.
4. **State enforcement is the last line.** With federal DOJ environmental cases down 95%, state agencies are the only ones still holding violators accountable.
5. **The extraction machine is accelerating, not slowing.** 2024-2025 saw record emissions, record temperatures, and record corporate backtracking.
